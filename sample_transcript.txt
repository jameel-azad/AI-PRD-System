ARJUN KAPOOR: Good morning everyone, thanks for making the time. We're here to capture all the requirements for the PhonePay super-app. Let's start with the big picture — Ravi, can you walk us through what you're trying to build?

RAVI SHANKAR: Sure. So PhonePay today is a simple peer-to-peer UPI app. We have about 8 million registered users, roughly 2.5 million monthly actives. The vision for this new version is to turn it into a complete financial super-app. We want to add credit cards, debit cards, BNPL — buy now pay later — wallet top-ups, utility bill payments, mobile recharges, insurance premium collection, and eventually a savings account integration. The big differentiator is a unified transaction inbox, so the user sees every money movement — whether it's UPI, card, wallet, EMI — in one timeline. That's the core of the experience.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Got it. And when you say 8 million users, are you expecting that to grow significantly after launch?

RAVI SHANKAR: We're targeting 25 million registered users within 18 months of launch. We're doing a heavy marketing push. Peak concurrent users — so simultaneous active sessions — we're expecting around 150,000 at the absolute peak. Think salary credit day, festival season like Diwali, end-of-month bill rushes. Those are the spikes we have to handle.

SUNITA RAO: When you say 150,000 concurrent, is that across all features or specifically the payment flow?

RAVI SHANKAR: Specifically the payment initiation and confirmation flow. The rest of the app — browsing, history — can be a bit slower, that's fine.

SUNITA RAO: And what's your acceptable latency for payment initiation?

RAVI SHANKAR: UPI transactions must complete end-to-end in under 4 seconds. Card transactions — because of the payment network overhead — we'll accept up to 8 seconds. But the user must see a clear "processing" indicator within 1 second of tapping Pay. That responsiveness is non-negotiable.

ANANYA IYER: I'd add that the payment status confirmation — the green tick or red X — must appear within 2 seconds of the bank's response. We've seen competitors take 10-15 seconds to show confirmation even after the bank has already processed it. That's a UX failure we want to avoid completely.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Noted. Priya, you want to walk us through the user flows?

PRIYA NAIR: Yes. So there are six primary user flows we've identified. First is UPI send money — user enters UPI ID or scans QR, enters amount, authenticates with UPI PIN, gets confirmation. Second is UPI collect request — user sends a collect request to another UPI ID, the recipient approves or declines, payer gets notified. Third is card payments — user stores a card, enters card number, expiry, CVD — and then pays. Cards must never be stored as raw numbers on our servers. We'll tokenize everything via Razorpay or a PCI-compliant vault. Fourth is wallet top-up and wallet payment. Fifth is bill payments — electricity, water, gas, DTH, broadband. We'll integrate with BBPS — the Bharat Bill Payment System — for this. Sixth is mobile recharge — prepaid top-up, plan purchase, data add-ons.

ARJUN KAPOOR: What about BNPL — buy now pay later?

PRIYA NAIR: That's a phase two feature. We want to launch with the six core flows, get stability, and then layer in BNPL in the second quarter post-launch. The platform architecture must support it being added though — the transaction model has to be extensible.

DEEPAK MEHTA: I need to jump in here on the compliance side because it affects architecture significantly. For card storage, we are absolutely PCI DSS Level 1 compliant — that means no raw PANs — primary account numbers — stored anywhere in our infrastructure. We tokenize at the point of entry. The tokenization must happen before the data ever hits our application layer. I've seen architectures where the PAN passes through the app backend before being sent to the vault — that's non-compliant and we cannot do that.

SUNITA RAO: Are you mandating a specific vault provider?

DEEPAK MEHTA: We have a preference for Razorpay Token HQ or MasterCard's MDES network. But architecturally, the integration point must be abstract enough that we can swap providers. Legal is still evaluating costs.

DEEPAK MEHTA: Second compliance item — KYC. Under RBI's new guidelines as of 2025, any wallet that holds more than 10,000 rupees requires full video KYC. Below that threshold, minimum KYC — just Aadhaar OTP — is sufficient. The system must track each user's KYC tier and enforce wallet balance limits dynamically. If a user's balance approaches 10,000 rupees we need to prompt them to complete video KYC or we block further top-ups.

ARJUN KAPOOR: What's the user experience for video KYC — is it in-app or redirected?

DEEPAK MEHTA: It must be in-app. We cannot redirect to an external URL for KYC — too much drop-off. We'll integrate with a vendor like Hyperverge or IDfy via their SDK. The SDK captures the selfie, ID document, and liveness check. Results come back via webhook. The system must handle async KYC results — the user shouldn't have to wait in the app.

PRIYA NAIR: And on that note, notifications. We need push notifications, in-app notifications, and SMS for all transactional events. Push and in-app are P0 — they must be present at launch. SMS is P1 — we'll handle that post-launch. Email notifications are P2.

RAVI SHANKAR: Every payment success and failure must trigger a push notification within 5 seconds of the event. Not eventually consistent — within 5 seconds. Our users complain about this constantly with our current app.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Okay. Let me ask about authentication. What's the auth model?

ANANYA IYER: Multi-factor. At registration, user enters mobile number, receives OTP, verifies. Then they set a 6-digit MPIN — Mobile PIN — for app login. The MPIN is separate from the UPI PIN. So the user has: MPIN to open the app, UPI PIN to authenticate payments. For cards, we add another layer — card CVV or OTP from the bank for the first transaction with a new card, and then tokenized for subsequent transactions.

RAVI SHANKAR: The MPIN must be stored as a salted hash. Never plaintext. Actually, nothing sensitive — MPIN, UPI PIN mappings, card tokens — should ever appear in application logs. This has to be enforced at the framework level.

DEEPAK MEHTA: Also, MPIN lockout policy: 5 failed attempts in a row locks the account for 24 hours and sends an SMS alert. Account unlock requires OTP on registered mobile number.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Sessions — what's the timeout policy?

ANANYA IYER: The app session expires after 10 minutes of inactivity. Background token refresh is fine, but if the user hasn't touched the app for 10 minutes, they re-enter MPIN to unlock. For web — if we ever build a web interface — it's the same but with a stricter 5-minute timeout given the browser environment.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Speaking of multi-platform — what platforms are you targeting?

RAVI SHANKAR: Android first. Minimum Android 8.0 — API level 26. iOS second, minimum iOS 14. We have a 60-40 Android-iOS user split currently, so Android gets more QA attention. No web app at launch, but the API must be designed so a web frontend can be built later without changes to the backend.

PRIYA NAIR: Also — language support. At launch we need English and Hindi. Within 6 months of launch we need to add Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Bengali, and Kannada. The copy system in the app must support runtime language switching — user can change language without restarting the app.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Let's talk about the transaction history and analytics — what does that look like?

PRIYA NAIR: The unified transaction inbox is a reverse-chronological timeline of all money movements. Every entry shows: counterparty name or logo, amount, transaction type — UPI/card/wallet/bill — status, timestamp, and reference number. User can tap any transaction to see full details including bank reference number, payment method details, and any failure reason if it failed.

RAVI SHANKAR: Filters are important. The user must be able to filter by: date range, transaction type, status — success/pending/failed — and amount range. The filter combination must persist across sessions — user sets a filter, closes the app, opens it again, the filter is still applied.

PRIYA NAIR: Search is also a P0 feature. Full-text search across transaction notes, payee names, and UPI IDs. Search results must return in under 2 seconds for up to 2 years of history.

ANANYA IYER: From a data perspective — we retain transaction data for 7 years to comply with FEMA and Income Tax regulations. The system architecture must account for this. Hot storage for the last 12 months, warm storage for months 13-36, cold archival for years 3-7. Retrieval from cold storage can take up to 30 seconds — that's acceptable, but the UI must show a "fetching older records" loading state.

ARJUN KAPOOR: What about disputes and refunds?

PRIYA NAIR: For UPI transactions, the user can raise a dispute within 30 days of the transaction. The dispute form captures: reason — transaction not received, wrong amount, merchant fraud — a description, and optionally a screenshot. The dispute gets assigned to our ops team and the user gets a case number. The user can track dispute status in-app. Resolution SLA is 7 business days per NPCI guidelines.

RAVI SHANKAR: For card transactions, the dispute goes to the card network — Visa or Mastercard — via the acquiring bank. We don't handle card chargebacks directly. But the user must be able to initiate the chargeback request through our app, and we pass it to Razorpay who routes it to the acquiring bank.

DEEPAK MEHTA: Refunds — automatic refunds for failed UPI transactions must be processed within T+1 business day as per RBI mandate. The system must track refund status and proactively notify the user if a refund is delayed beyond T+3.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Let's cover the merchant-side features.

RAVI SHANKAR: We have two merchant tiers. Small merchants — think a chai stall — they just need a static QR code and a simple dashboard showing recent payments. Medium merchants — think a retail store — they need a POS integration, dynamic QR generation per transaction, daily settlement reports, and GST invoice generation. Large merchants are out of scope for phase one — that's an enterprise product we'll build separately.

PRIYA NAIR: For merchant onboarding, small merchants complete a simplified KYC — GST number or Udyam registration, bank account for settlement, PAN card. Medium merchants go through full GST verification, partnership deed or incorporation certificate, and two-day manual review by our merchant ops team.

SUNITA RAO: What's the settlement cycle for merchants?

RAVI SHANKAR: Small merchants: T+2 settlement. Medium merchants: T+1. Both must receive a settlement notification via SMS and email with a transaction-level breakdown. The settlement report must be downloadable as PDF and Excel.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Now let's talk about fraud and risk.

ANANYA IYER: We need real-time fraud scoring for every payment. Every transaction above 1000 rupees gets a risk score computed before the payment is routed to the bank. Score computation must complete in under 200 milliseconds — it cannot be on the payment critical path in a way that slows down the transaction. If the score is above 0.8, we auto-decline and flag for review. If it's between 0.5 and 0.8, we add a step-up authentication — user gets OTP in addition to UPI PIN. Below 0.5, normal flow.

RAVI SHANKAR: The fraud model inputs are: device fingerprint, transaction velocity — how many transactions in the last 10 minutes, 1 hour, 24 hours — amount deviation from user's historical average, payee reputation — is this UPI ID associated with known fraud, and geolocation anomaly — is the user suddenly transacting from a different city than usual.

ANANYA IYER: Device fingerprint must be stable across app reinstalls using the same hardware. We'll use a combination of hardware identifiers that survive reinstallation. The device fingerprint must be stored encrypted and must not include data that uniquely identifies the user to a third party without consent under the DPDP Act — the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023.

DEEPAK MEHTA: DPDP compliance is a hard requirement. Every piece of personal data we collect needs a stated purpose. Users must be able to request a copy of all their data within 72 hours — that's a data portability request. Users must be able to request deletion of their account and data — right to be forgotten — except for data we're legally required to retain like transaction records under FEMA. The system must track consent records — when did the user consent to what — with timestamps and version numbers of our privacy policy.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Admin dashboard — what does your ops team need?

PRIYA NAIR: The ops dashboard is a separate web application, not part of the consumer app. It needs: user management — look up any user by mobile or email, view their KYC status, transaction history, account status. Transaction monitoring — view all transactions in real-time with filters, flag suspicious ones, initiate manual refunds. Dispute management — see all open disputes, assign them, update status, communicate with the user. Merchant management — approve, suspend, set limits. And a metrics dashboard — daily active users, transaction volumes, success rates, average transaction value, top failure reasons.

RAVI SHANKAR: The ops dashboard must have role-based access. Roles: Super Admin — full access including user deletion. Ops Manager — all except user deletion. Customer Support Agent — read-only transaction history, can raise refunds up to 10,000 rupees. Compliance Officer — can export data for regulatory reports, read-only everything else.

SUNITA RAO: Are there any existing internal systems the ops dashboard needs to integrate with?

ANANYA IYER: Yes — our Jira instance for dispute ticket creation, our Slack workspace for fraud alerts, and our existing Tableau dashboards. Jira and Slack integration at launch. Tableau — we'll export CSV data and they can build dashboards themselves, that's phase two.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Let's talk infrastructure and scalability.

ANANYA IYER: We're going cloud-native on AWS. Primary region: ap-south-1 — Mumbai. Disaster recovery region: ap-southeast-1 — Singapore. RTO — Recovery Time Objective — is 4 hours. RPO — Recovery Point Objective — is 15 minutes. The DR region must have a warm standby that can take traffic within 30 minutes of a Mumbai outage.

SUNITA RAO: Are you considering multi-region active-active or active-passive?

ANANYA IYER: Active-passive for now. Active-active adds complexity we're not ready for. But the architecture must not preclude moving to active-active in the future.

RAVI SHANKAR: 99.9% uptime SLA for the payment API. That's about 8.7 hours of allowed downtime per year. Planned maintenance windows must be between 2 AM and 5 AM IST and must be pre-announced to users via in-app banner 48 hours in advance.

ANANYA IYER: Database — we're going with PostgreSQL on RDS with read replicas. The primary handles all writes. Read replicas handle transaction history queries, analytics, search. Auto-scaling on the application tier. The payment processing service must be stateless so it can scale horizontally. No sticky sessions anywhere in the payment flow.

ARJUN KAPOOR: What about message queues and async processing?

ANANYA IYER: We're using SQS for webhook processing — bank callbacks, NPCI notifications. All webhooks are consumed off a queue, not processed synchronously. Dead letter queue for failed webhook processing with a 3-retry policy. Failed webhooks after 3 retries get escalated to PagerDuty. We're also considering Kafka for the real-time fraud scoring pipeline — we need sub-200ms scoring and Kafka gives us the throughput.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Let's discuss the API design.

ANANYA IYER: RESTful APIs with JSON. API versioning from day one — /api/v1/ prefix. Rate limiting: 100 requests per minute per authenticated user, 10 requests per minute per IP for unauthenticated endpoints. The payment initiation endpoint has a separate rate limit of 10 requests per minute per user — we don't want anyone able to spam transactions. All APIs must return standardized error responses with a machine-readable error code and human-readable message.

SUNITA RAO: Are you doing a BFF — Backend for Frontend — pattern or a single API?

ANANYA IYER: Single API at launch, but designed so we can introduce a BFF layer later. The consumer app and the merchant app will hit the same API but with different JWT token scopes. Admin dashboard will have its own authentication and a separate set of admin-only endpoints.

RAVI SHANKAR: API documentation is a hard requirement. We use Swagger/OpenAPI. Every endpoint must be documented including error codes. The Swagger UI must be available in the staging environment. Production must not expose Swagger UI publicly.

ARJUN KAPOOR: Now let me ask about integration with banking partners and NPCI.

ANANYA IYER: We're a Payment Service Provider — PSP — not a bank. We go through a sponsor bank for UPI connectivity. Our sponsor bank is ICICI Bank. The NPCI UPI switch connection is managed by ICICI on our behalf. The integration is via APIs with ICICI's payment gateway. We receive callbacks for transaction status via HTTPS webhooks.

RAVI SHANKAR: For card acquiring, we're using Razorpay as the payment gateway. Card payment flow: user enters card details, Razorpay tokenizes, we store the token with Razorpay's vault reference, all subsequent payments use the token. We never see the PAN after the initial tokenization step.

DEEPAK MEHTA: For international cards — Visa and Mastercard international cards — there's an additional 3D Secure step. The 3DS authentication must be embedded in-app using the 3DS SDK — not a browser redirect. That's a hard requirement from our card network agreements.

ARJUN KAPOOR: What about the BBPS integration for bill payments?

PRIYA NAIR: We connect to BBPS via a BBPS Operating Unit — we're using BillJunction as our BBPS OU. The integration is via their API. Supported biller categories at launch: electricity — all major DISCOMs, broadband — Jio, Airtel, BSNL, DTH — Tata Play, Dish TV, Airtel Digital TV, mobile recharge — all operators, piped gas — Mahanagar Gas and Indraprastha Gas, water — municipal corporations in Tier 1 cities. We will expand to more billers post-launch but the integration architecture must support adding new billers without code changes — just configuration.

RAVI SHANKAR: Biller fetch — the user enters their consumer ID and we fetch their outstanding bill amount before they pay. The fetch must complete in under 3 seconds. If the biller's API is slow, we show a cached bill amount from the last successful fetch with a "last updated" timestamp. Never block the user from paying due to a slow biller API.

RAVI SHANKAR: Onboarding must be completable in under 2 minutes for a typical user. The flow: download app, enter mobile number, OTP, set MPIN, add UPI ID, optional KYC prompt. The KYC prompt is skippable at this stage — we ask again when the wallet balance approaches the threshold.

PRIYA NAIR: Offboarding — account deletion. The user must be able to delete their account in-app. The deletion is soft-delete initially — 30 days to reverse. After 30 days, hard delete of personal data but transaction records are retained per FEMA compliance. During the 30-day grace period, the user's UPI ID is deactivated — they cannot send or receive — but their transaction history remains visible to them.

ARJUN KAPOOR: What analytics and monitoring do you need from an operational perspective?

ANANYA IYER: Application performance monitoring via AWS CloudWatch and Datadog.
